The three things to know

01 · Physiology

Your heart rate naturally oscillates with your breath

Inhale, it speeds up. Exhale, it slows. This is respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) — a direct readout of vagus-nerve activity. A bigger RSA oscillation means better autonomic flexibility. More on RSA →

02 · Resonance

One breathing pace makes that oscillation enormous

Your cardiovascular system has a resonant frequency — set by the baroreflex loop between your heart and arteries. Breathe there and the RSA oscillation amplifies dramatically. Common questions

Is there an app to find my resonance breathing pace?

Yes — Resonance: HRV Biofeedback is a free Android app built specifically for this. It runs a 12-minute guided assessment using any Bluetooth heart-rate sensor — chest strap, arm band, or smartwatch — testing candidate breathing rates against your live HRV to converge on the exact pace that peaks your heart-rate variability. Once your pace is stored, you can practice without any hardware. Available on Google Play.

What is resonance frequency breathing?

Resonance frequency breathing means breathing at the exact pace that matches the natural oscillation frequency of your cardiovascular baroreflex loop. At that pace, respiratory sinus arrhythmia amplifies dramatically — your heart-rate variability peaks, and the HRV signal becomes a single clean oscillation (coherence). For most adults this falls between 4.5 and 6.5 breaths per minute, but the precise value is individual.

Why isn't 6 breaths per minute right for everyone?

"6 BPM" is a population average from early research, not an individual prescription. Your resonance frequency is set by the delay in your own baroreflex loop — the feedback system between blood pressure and heart rate. That delay varies with body size, age, and cardiovascular fitness. Using the wrong pace produces significantly less coherence. Individual resonance frequencies range from roughly 4.5 to 7 BPM.

Do I need a sensor? Does it have to be a chest strap?

No sensor is required for practice sessions at your saved breathing pace, and when one is needed it does not have to be a chest strap. A Bluetooth heart-rate sensor is only needed when you want live HRV biofeedback — during the initial 12-minute assessment to find your pace, and during sessions where you want to track your coherence score in real time. The app connects to any device broadcasting the standard Bluetooth Heart Rate Profile, including GPS smartwatches from Garmin, Polar, Wahoo, Samsung, Amazfit, and Fitbit (enable heart-rate broadcast in the watch's settings), optical arm bands, and Apple Watch via a free bridge app like Wristly. Dedicated chest straps (Polar H10, Polar H9, Garmin HRM-Pro, Garmin HRM-Dual, Wahoo TICKR) give the richest raw beat-to-beat data, but they're not required — see the device connection guide for setup steps.

How is this different from HeartMath?

HeartMath uses a proprietary single-number coherence score and prescribes a general breathing pace. Resonance: HRV Biofeedback measures your individual resonance frequency, shows all six Shaffer coherence criteria in real time, works with any standard Bluetooth heart-rate sensor — chest strap, arm band, or smartwatch — rather than proprietary hardware, and is completely free — no purchase or subscription required.

How long until I see results?

Eight to ten weeks of 20-minute daily sessions at your resonance frequency have repeatedly produced measurable improvements in baseline HRV, baroreflex sensitivity, and vagal tone in clinical research. This is a physiological training protocol — it changes your autonomic nervous system over time, not just in the moment.
