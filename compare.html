Who each app is actually for

Resonance: HRV Biofeedback

The only app in this category that combines all of the following: finds your personal resonance frequency with an adaptive iterative protocol, exposes the full Shaffer six-criteria coherence model in real time, produces a decomposed Session Quality Score after each session so you can track genuine improvement over weeks, and costs nothing. No subscription, no account, no proprietary hardware. Your heart rate data never leaves your device.

Most scientifically rigorous tools in this space have dense, utilitarian interfaces — functional but not something you'd want to open every day. Most polished, beautiful apps have stripped the science out entirely. Resonance: HRV Biofeedback is built around the idea that these don't have to be in tension. The interface is clean and intuitive — a daily breathing session requires no manual, no configuration overhead, and no digging through menus. At the same time, the full spectral analysis, six coherence criteria, heart-rate trace, RSA amplitude, RMSSD, and multi-dimensional Quality Score are all one tap away for users who want to understand what's happening physiologically. Approachable enough for a first-time user. Precise enough for a researcher or clinician.

The target user is anyone who takes the physiology seriously — who wants to know why a breathing pace is right for them, see the signal that justifies the result, and have a quantified measure of whether their practice is improving. And who expects the tool they use every day to actually look good.

RHz Biofeedback

The closest direct competitor. RHz Biofeedback runs a guided sweep across different breathing paces while connected to a Bluetooth heart-rate monitor — the same core concept as the ASSESS phase here. The meaningful differences: Resonance: HRV Biofeedback uses a three-phase adaptive protocol (ASSESS → REFINE → HOLD) that iterates to a precise result rather than a single linear pass. All six Shaffer coherence criteria are visible throughout every session, so you can verify why the protocol landed on your specific frequency — not just trust a final number. And it produces a Session Quality Score after every training session, so you have a quantified benchmark to improve against week over week.

PreciseBreath

Also targeting resonance frequency via Bluetooth chest strap, with "Explore" and "Calibrate" modes. The central question for any resonance-frequency app is whether it shows you enough of the underlying signal to verify the result — or whether you're just trusting the output. Resonance: HRV Biofeedback exposes the full heart-rate trace, frequency spectrum, and all six Shaffer coherence criteria throughout, plus a Session Quality Score that decomposes your performance into three auditable components. PreciseBreath's methodology is not publicly documented.

HeartMath Inner Balance

HeartMath has brand recognition and a large body of published research — both real advantages, especially in clinical settings. But there are three significant limitations worth knowing. First, the Inner Balance sensor costs around $129 and is required; no standard Bluetooth sensor works. Add a subscription and the cost of getting started is $200+. Second, HeartMath prescribes a breathing pace — it doesn't measure yours. The default is 6 BPM, which is a population average; your actual resonance frequency may be meaningfully different, and training at the wrong pace produces substantially less coherence. Third, the "coherence" score is a proprietary single number that doesn't correspond to any published multi-criteria model, so you can't evaluate what it's actually measuring.

If you're a clinician who needs a brand your clients recognise, or you want the most turnkey possible experience and price is no object, HeartMath is a reasonable choice. For everyone else, the combination of cost, lock-in, and measurement opacity is hard to justify when a free, open-methodology alternative exists.

Elite HRV (now HRV4Training Pro)

Built for athletes tracking daily readiness via morning HRV measurements. If your primary goal is monitoring recovery and avoiding overtraining, Elite HRV is the better tool. For active resonance frequency training with live biofeedback, the breathing module is secondary to their core readiness product and doesn't offer the same depth.

Welltory

Consumer-friendly HRV trend tracker that pulls summary data from Apple Watch, Garmin, or Fitbit's own wearable API after the fact — not a live beat-by-beat stream. That's fine for retrospective trend tracking, but real-time biofeedback needs to see the signal while you're breathing. Resonance: HRV Biofeedback reads a live Bluetooth heart-rate broadcast instead — from a chest strap or from a GPS smartwatch with heart-rate broadcast enabled — giving real-time feedback during the session itself. Welltory is useful as a lifestyle trend indicator alongside a dedicated biofeedback tool; not a substitute for one.

Breathwrk

A good guided breathing app for people who want structured patterns — box breathing, 4-7-8, Wim Hof. It has no heart-rate sensor integration, so it cannot tell you whether the breathing is producing coherence, how large your RSA swings are, or whether your practice is improving by any physiological measure. Without biofeedback, you're following a timer, not training a response. Resonance: HRV Biofeedback also supports sensor-free practice sessions once your frequency is stored — so a sensor (chest strap, arm band, or smartwatch) is only needed when you want live measurement, not every session.

Honest limitations

  • Android-only for now. iOS is planned but not yet released. If you're on iPhone, HeartMath or Elite HRV are currently your options.
  • No automatic cloud sync — by design, because heart rate data is sensitive and we don't want it on a third-party server. The tradeoff: if you switch phones, you need to export your sessions first. The built-in backup exports a plain JSON file you control entirely; import takes one tap on the new device.
  • Breadth of breathing patterns. Breathwrk has a larger library of varied breathing styles (box, 4-7-8, Wim Hof). Resonance: HRV Biofeedback includes a breathing practices library with multiple modes, but its focus is resonance training — not general breathwork variety.
  • No streaks or gamification. Sessions, scores, and data. That's deliberate, but if social features or achievement systems motivate you, this isn't the app for that.
The take

If your goal is to find your personal resonance frequency, train at it with live biofeedback, and have objective evidence that your practice is actually improving your coherence over weeks — Resonance: HRV Biofeedback is the strongest tool available for that job, and it costs nothing. The Shaffer six-criteria methodology is fully exposed and published; the Session Quality Score gives you three independent dimensions of improvement to track; your data stays on your device. No other app in this category offers all of that.

HeartMath is the right choice if you need a brand name your clinician recognises and price is secondary. Elite HRV is the right choice if daily readiness tracking is your primary goal. Breathwrk is the right choice if you want variety in breathing styles without physiological measurement. For everything else — finding your frequency and training it with real biofeedback — this is where we'd point you.
