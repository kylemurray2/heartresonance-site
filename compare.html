Who each app is actually for

RHz Biofeedback

The closest direct competitor in the resonance-frequency niche. RHz Biofeedback runs a guided sweep across different breathing paces while connected to a Bluetooth heart-rate monitor, which is the same core concept as the ASSESS protocol here. If you've tried RHz Biofeedback and found the analysis too opaque, the main differentiator with Resonance: HRV Biofeedback is the Shaffer six-criteria display — all six coherence metrics are visible in real time, not hidden behind a single summary score. Resonance: HRV Biofeedback also uses an adaptive three-phase protocol (ASSESS → REFINE → HOLD) rather than a single linear sweep, which converges to a more precise result. Both apps target the same job; the methodology transparency and convergence algorithm are the meaningful differences.

PreciseBreath

Also targeting resonance frequency measurement via Bluetooth chest strap, with "Explore" and "Calibrate" modes that map to roughly the same two phases of the protocol (finding a candidate frequency, then training at it). If PreciseBreath's UI or ecosystem fits you better, it's a legitimate tool for the same job. The key question for any resonance-frequency app is: does it show you the underlying HRV data so you can verify the result yourself? Resonance: HRV Biofeedback exposes the full heart-rate trace, frequency spectrum, and Shaffer criteria throughout — so you're not trusting a number without being able to see the signal behind it.

HeartMath Inner Balance

The category leader by a long way. HeartMath has been doing coherence training since the 1990s and the research base behind their methodology is extensive and respected. The Inner Balance sensor and app are used in clinical settings worldwide. The downsides are the proprietary sensor, the subscription model, and a single coherence metric that obscures what's going on underneath. If you want a turnkey clinical-grade experience and don't mind the cost, this is the default choice.

Elite HRV (now HRV4Training Pro)

Built for athletes tracking daily readiness via morning HRV measurements, with a secondary breathing-training feature. If your primary use case is monitoring overtraining and recovery, Elite HRV is the better tool. For coherence training specifically, the breathing module is functional but not the main thing they invest in.

Welltory

Consumer-friendly app built on wrist-PPG HRV from Apple Watch, Garmin, or Fitbit. Pretty charts, lifestyle framing, daily "stress" score. Useful as a trend tracker if you're already wearing a watch. Not accurate enough at the beat level to be the core tool for resonance training. Treat it as a companion, not a substitute.

Breathwrk

An excellent guided breathing app — box breathing, 4-7-8, Wim Hof, various specific protocols. Not an HRV tool at all. If you want coached breathwork across a range of styles and the science of measurement isn't your priority, Breathwrk is good. But it can't tell you whether the breathing is actually doing what the literature says it does — for that you need heart-rate feedback. Resonance: HRV Biofeedback also works without a sensor connected (practice mode at your stored frequency), so a chest strap is needed only when you want live coherence measurement — not every time you breathe.

Resonance: HRV Biofeedback

Built specifically around Vaschillo / Lehrer-style resonance frequency training, measured directly from your HRV, with the Shaffer six-criteria methodology fully exposed. No cloud, no cost, standard Bluetooth sensors. Each session ends with a Session Quality Score — a 0–100 composite of time in coherence, RSA amplitude, and consistency — that separates real physiological improvement from lucky single-session peaks. Session history tracks trends across all three dimensions with color-coded charts. Shareable session cards let you export a visual summary (with your actual heart-rate trace) via the system share sheet.

The sweet spot: anyone who wants the measurement rigor of Elite HRV and the dedicated resonance-frequency focus of HeartMath, without a subscription, proprietary hardware, or a cloud account. All data stays on your device; the optional backup exports a plain JSON file you control entirely.

Fair criticisms of Resonance: HRV Biofeedback

To return the fairness: a few honest weaknesses vs. the competition.

  • Less polished UX in places. HeartMath has been iterating on this for 20+ years. We've been iterating for months.
  • No cloud sync. By design, but if you change phones you need to export your sessions first. Cloud sync is on the roadmap if user demand supports it without compromising the privacy stance.
  • Android-only at launch. iOS is planned but not yet available. If you're on iPhone, HeartMath or Elite HRV are your current options.
  • Breathwork breadth. Breathwrk has a larger library of coached breathing patterns across many styles (box, 4-7-8, Wim Hof, etc.). Resonance: HRV Biofeedback focuses on resonance training — it includes a breathing practices library with multiple modes, but breadth of pattern variety is not the primary goal.
  • No built-in community or gamification. Just sessions, scores, and data. Some people want streaks and badges; we've kept it minimal.
The take

There is not a single "best" app in this space — they aim at different jobs. For finding your personal resonance frequency with a Bluetooth chest strap, the three dedicated options are Resonance: HRV Biofeedback, RHz Biofeedback, and PreciseBreath. The differentiators are methodology transparency, how much of the underlying HRV signal is visible to you, and whether the app gives you the tools to track genuine improvement over weeks — not just a live coherence indicator during a single session. Resonance: HRV Biofeedback is the only one of the three that produces a decomposed Session Quality Score after each session and shows your trend in time-in-coherence, RSA amplitude, and consistency side by side. If you want a clinically-established brand and don't mind the cost and lock-in, HeartMath is great. If you want daily readiness tracking, Elite HRV. If you want coached breathwork without the measurement layer, Breathwrk. Pick the job, then pick the tool.
